Key stories

Markets

The AI sell-off spreads from stocks to the AI labs

On 5 Nov the S&P 500 fell 4.4%, its worst day since April 2025, and the Nasdaq 5.5%. AI data centre stocks led the fall, on worries that expensive borrowing and the midterm results could slow the build-out (AP). Earlier in the week the WSJ reported that Elliott warned investors the risks it sees could trigger a severe market correction, with debt-funded AI spending among them (Hedgeweek).

The labs felt it too: OpenAI closed $11bn at a $1.25trn valuation, below the $1.4trn it first sought, Bloomberg reported, and Reuters reported investors are questioning Anthropic's IPO valuation and timing.

Why it mattersMany of our clients have lent to or invested in the AI build-out, so this is a good moment to talk to them about their exposure.

Hedge funds

Hedge funds hit by the AI rout

In one week, hedge funds sold almost two years' worth of AI stock buying, on doubts about valuations and whether AI spending will last, Goldman Sachs prime brokerage data showed (Hedgeweek). October was painful. Engineers Gate, a Moody's client, was down 5.5% by 23 Oct as AI and tech stocks reversed, and cut most of its fundamental equities team (Hedgeweek). In Asia, Aspex lost 16%, Bloomberg reported (Hedgeweek). Walleye Capital ($17bn) lost 5.9%, its worst month since March 2020, and is restructuring its equity long/short business (Hedgeweek) and plans to return some capital (FT).

Why it mattersHedge funds are a big part of our client base. When they lose money, cut teams and hand capital back, every cost line gets reviewed, data and AI tools included.

Survey

PwC: fragmented data slows AI at asset managers

In a PwC report drawing on 150 asset managers and 50 infrastructure providers, 24% named data fragmentation as a major barrier to scaling AI, and 44% favour mixing build, buy and partner. Profit per assets under management is down 19% since 2018.

Why it mattersManagers want to buy and partner, not build everything, and fragmented data holds them back.

AI platforms

AI workspaces pitch themselves as the layer above data vendors' MCPs

AlphaSense launched an MCP for its SuperAnalyst agent for teams that otherwise license a separate MCP from each data vendor. Rogo says its structured company profiles (Rogo Intelligence) cut agent steps by 42% compared with connectors, in about 8,800 tasks. Hebbia is now available in Microsoft Copilot Cowork through its MCP.

Private credit · Q3 call

Apollo: "Data drives everything."

On Apollo's Q3 call, CEO Marc Rowan said Apollo will spend roughly $350m over the next 24 months on data and systems to make private credit easier to price and trade, and will write off its old software. President Jim Zelter put AI and digital infrastructure at about 2.5% to 3% of Apollo.

Why it mattersOne of the largest private credit managers is saying the edge is data.

Traditional managers

Franklin Templeton: results changed once it added a semantic layer

Franklin Templeton, a global investment management firm, first put its data in a database and connected AI models to it. It didn't produce the answers the teams needed, as the models didn't understand how the data fit together. When the team added agents and a semantic layer, the results changed, according to OpenAI's case study.

Why it mattersEven with a frontier model, the data underneath decides whether the answers are right. More in the concept below.

KYC

BCG shares lessons from an 11-agent KYC build on Anthropic's tools

BCG built a reference KYC onboarding system on Anthropic's KYC Screener. The system had 11 agents, 17 skills and three MCP connections to data, and a person makes the final decision.

Why it mattersA KYC agent is only as good as the entity and ownership data it calls, and that is our data.

Concept of the week

The semantic layer

A semantic layer, which Franklin Templeton added alongside its agents (see above), is a business-friendly layer between complex data and the people who use it: it standardises business definitions and metrics across the organisation, so every report and tool calculates them the same way.

Think of a new analyst's first week: before touching the portfolio, someone explains what 'exposure', 'NAV' and 'leverage' mean at this firm and which system holds what. A semantic layer gives the AI that same induction.

You'll hear this from clients when they say their AI gives different answers to the same question, or that the hard part is getting it to understand their own data.
